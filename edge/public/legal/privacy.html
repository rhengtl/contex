3. Where your document goes

Your document is transmitted to a third-party AI provider — currently Google, through the Gemini API — which reads it and returns LaTeX. Specifically:

  • The file is uploaded to Google's Files API and then referenced in the conversion request. ConTeX deletes the uploaded file as soon as the reply arrives. Until that delete completes, and if it fails, the file is subject to Google's own retention for uploaded files.
  • The file is sent as you chose it. It is not resized, re-encoded or reduced first.
  • A PDF is split in your browser and sent one page at a time, up to 10 pages. Pages beyond the tenth are not converted and are not sent.
  • Canvas drawings and camera photographs are sent as images.

ConTeX runs on Google's free tier, and that has consequences

This is the single most important thing in this policy. Under the Gemini API Additional Terms of Service (last updated 28 April 2026), for the unpaid services:

  • Google “uses the content you submit to the Services and any generated responses to provide, improve, and develop Google products and services and machine learning technologies” — so both your document and the LaTeX produced from it may be used to train Google's models.
  • “Human reviewers may read, annotate, and process your API input and output.” Google says it disconnects that data from the account, API key and project first.
  • Google states no retention period for content used this way, and deleting the uploaded file does not withdraw it. Our delete removes the file from the Files API; it does not reach anything already taken for product improvement.

Google's own instruction is: “Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services.” Please treat that as this Service's instruction too. If ConTeX is ever moved to a paid tier, Google does not use prompts or responses to improve its products, and this notice will be changed to say so.

ConTeX keeps a small record of whether the AI service is currently failing, so it can warn the next person. That record contains only a status code and a timestamp — never any part of anyone's document.

4. What happens in your browser and nowhere else

This build does more on your own device than a server-rendered one can, and the difference is a privacy difference rather than only a technical one:

  • The PDF preview is compiled on your device. A complete LaTeX engine and font set are downloaded as ordinary static files and run in the page. The generated .tex is never sent anywhere to be compiled.
  • The generated .tex is never stored on a server unless you are signed in and it is saved to your history.
  • Splitting a PDF into pages happens on your device.
  • Guest history never leaves your browser.
  • The offline conversion sends your document nowhere at all. When the AI service is unavailable, ConTeX offers to convert your document on your own device instead. If you accept, the text and formula recognisers are downloaded as ordinary static files (about 50 MB, once) and run inside the page. Your document is not uploaded to Google or to us, and no part of it is sent to any third party. It is also the lower-quality path, and ConTeX says so before you choose it — it is never taken without your agreement, and your agreement applies only to that one conversion.

Those recognisers are served from this site and not from anyone else's. That is deliberate: the software they are built from normally fetches its language data from a third-party CDN, which would tell that CDN when you used the offline path. This build's content-security policy forbids the page from contacting anything except the services listed in section 6, so that request cannot happen.

6. Who else receives your data

Services ConTeX sends data to on the server side:

  • Google — Gemini API (including the Files API). Receives your document to convert it. Subject to the free-tier training note in Section 3.
  • Google — Firebase Authentication. Handles sign-up, sign-in, password reset and Google sign-in. Holds your email address, display name and password credentials.
  • Google — Cloud Firestore. Stores your profile and your saved history.
  • Cloudflare. Hosts the Service and serves every page and file. It processes your IP address and request metadata as a hosting provider, and stores the abuse-prevention counters described in Section 2.

Services your browser may contact directly. Loading an ordinary ConTeX page contacts nobody but Cloudflare: the pages, scripts, stylesheet and typefaces are all served from ConTeX's own address. The only exception is signing in with Google:

  • www.gstatic.com and apis.google.com — the Firebase JavaScript library and the sign-in frame, loaded only when you press Continue with Google on the sign-in or sign-up page.
  • identitytoolkit.googleapis.com, securetoken.googleapis.com and your project's firebaseapp.com address — contacted by that library during Google sign-in.

These receive your IP address and browser user-agent because your browser requests files from them — not because ConTeX sends them anything about you. They never receive your documents. The LaTeX engine, its fonts, the PDF renderer and the PDF splitter are all served from ConTeX's own address, so using them contacts nobody else.

Each of these providers processes what it receives under its own privacy terms, which apply in addition to this policy. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

7. International transfers

Google's and Cloudflare's services operate globally, so your document and your account data may be processed on servers outside the Philippines and outside your own country, including in the United States. Where personal data of people in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom is transferred, both providers state that they rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and their own adequacy arrangements.

ConTeX is a personal project and has not itself entered into transfer agreements with these providers beyond accepting their standard terms. If that matters for your data, do not upload it.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have some or all of the rights below. ConTeX applies them to everyone who asks, regardless of location: to be informed, to access a copy of the personal data held about you, to correct data that is inaccurate, to have it erased, to object to processing based on a legitimate interest, to restrict processing, and to receive your data in a portable form.

Deleting a saved conversion is self-service. Each entry in your history has a Delete control, and using it removes the record from the database. The server checks the record is yours before it deletes anything, and a record belonging to somebody else is answered exactly as one that does not exist.

For anything wider than that — a copy of your data, deleting your whole account, or correcting a profile field — write to the address in Section 1 and the operator will do it by hand. There is no charge, and no account is needed to ask.

10. Children

ConTeX is not intended for anyone under 16. If you believe someone under 16 has created an account, tell us and we will delete it and its data.

11. Security

Traffic is served over HTTPS only. The session cookie is signed and marked HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite=Lax. Saved history is scoped to its owner's user ID by the server on every read, so one account cannot reach another's documents. Passwords are held by Firebase and are never seen by ConTeX. No system is perfectly secure, and ConTeX is a personal project rather than an audited service.
